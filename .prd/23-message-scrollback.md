## Measured facts (60f92c6)

- The message window is the record `WindowId::Messages`, cells 22..=38 × 14..=23 (`ui/textstate.rs:170`), so pixels 176..=311 × 112..=191, in both builds (spec 13: the UI keeps its 320 × 200 coordinates; the page crops exactly this rect, `site/app.js:1588` `messages: from [176, 112, 136, 80]`, and Wide's 메시지 window shows the same region, `site/app.js:2127`).
- Text reaches the window's cells at five draw sites, all through `text::draw_cell` (`ui/text.rs:276`): `print::cell` (`ui/print.rs:29-35`: the assembled line, the centre padding, the more prompt's blank row), the key wait's cursor symbol and its erase (`ui/keyread.rs:149`, `:179`), the line editor's Backspace blank and its typed echo (`ui/keyread.rs:223`, `:225`).
- The window scrolls at two sites, both `text::scroll_window` of the current window (`ui/print.rs:276`, `ui/keyread.rs:235`; `ui/text.rs:446`): the top row moves out of the rect and is lost (0x10525c), the bottom row is filled with the window's paper.
- The paper under the rect, as the boot draws it, is the window's paper colour on 10 878 of its 10 880 pixels (probe, counts only).
- `u6.ch` inks the rightmost pixel column of 13 of its 95 printable glyphs and the next one of 76; the site's Hangul table never inks the rightmost column (probe, counts only).
- In a real conversation run (12 NPCs near the start, name / job / bye), 42 of 477 window rows hold text in their 17th cell (probe, counts only). A scrollbar that took the last cell column would hide a letter on those rows.
- The game's own scroll indicators are the arrow glyphs 0x18 / 0x19 printed in the inventory list (`inventory/panel.rs:302-305`), one cell each; its 1-pixel ink rules on the paper are the command underline (`ui/cursor.rs:33`, `:39`).
- The frame the player sees is composed by the engine: the published frame (`Io::published`, synced from the screen at a present) and the pointer sprite over it into `Engine::shown` (`engine.rs:854-860`; the source-only publish `:861-883`). The HD frame is composed from the published frame without the sprite (`hd.rs:150-169`). Game code reads the published frame (`combat/present.rs:376`), so the view is never drawn into it.

## Decisions (reversible; the owner vetoes on review)

1. **The history is cells, kept as the game draws them.** A shadow of the message window's rows (`ui::scrollback::Scrollback`, in `UiState`) records each cell a draw site writes inside the rect: code, ink, paper and kind (character, typed, symbol). When the message window scrolls, its top row goes to the history before the move, with the pixels of the screen row the cells do not reproduce (a colour patch; it makes every kept line colour-exact whatever drew there, with one exception, named: a keyword drawn inverse under the pointer is kept as drawn, in its ink, 10; rare: in the real-data receipt 36 of 1058 kept lines carry one, 2 pixels each), and the keywords it showed (spec 22's spans, 10). At most **500 lines**, oldest dropped first, each shared (`Rc<Line>`): a copy of the state, and the view's lines (2), copy a pointer a line. The memory is decision 11. It is presentation only: never saved, kept across a Restore, the same in every build.
2. **The view is the engine's, not the game's.** The offset (lines back from the live bottom, 0 = live, at most the lines it holds), the gesture and the kept lines as of the last publish (the shared lines, taken at each publish where lines were kept since the one before, with their history's token) live in `Engine` (`View`), not in `State`: the game reads none of it. What the view changes for the game is the input of a press it takes (6, 9: queued with no button held) and one thing more, spec 22's keyword targets (talk's hover and click, the exception): the engine copies the offset into the `Io` (`Io::history_offset`, written once a tick, after its input and before the game's pass, the one time the game reads it: the last publish's anchoring and a wheel since are in it, so a pass reads it as the player sees the window then; round 5 dropped the writes after the publish and at the wheel, which no pass read and nothing else does, the page asking the view, opus N3), and while it is not 0 `talk::pointer` takes no keyword of the message window as a target. The window then shows kept lines, not the keywords under the pointer: a keyword drawn inverse is drawn as it was at the next pointer event (the game draws that while scrolled back), and the buttons below the portrait, outside the window, are targets as before. A click across the change is dropped: a press on a keyword at the live bottom, held while the wheel goes back, then released types nothing (the release finds no keyword under it, `talk::pointer`, opus N4); the press is spec 22's, the release the history's. Between publishes the view draws its own lines, so lines kept or dropped after the published frame do not move it (a source-only publish at the bound included). Each history has a token (`Generation`: every new `Scrollback` has its own, and so does every copy of one; the one the game draws into moves with the state and is never copied); at a publish another token (a new game's state, as a relaunch makes in `main_loop::take_over`, of any length, or a copy put back) returns the view to the live bottom, and the view takes that history's lines. At offset 0 nothing is drawn, so the frame is the published frame (S3).
3. **The view is drawn between the published frame and the pointer sprite**, into `Engine::shown`, after its sync and before the sprite; the next sync puts back what it drew (the overlay's writes mark their chunks). For HD a second frame is built only while the offset is not 0, on demand: the published frame copied and the view drawn over it (`Engine::with_view_frame`); at offset 0 HD composes the published frame as before.
4. **The wheel.** `Engine::wheel(x, y, dy)`, outside the game's input queue (no new `InputEvent`): `dy` < 0 shows older lines, by |dy| lines; > 0 newer; 0 moves nothing and answers whether the wheel is taken there. It acts only over the message window's pixel rect, only while the program is `game`, and clamps to the lines kept. The page: an event with no vertical delta (`deltaY` 0, a horizontal swipe, in any mode) is the browser's: it returns right after the Ctrl check, before the engine is asked, before the carry and with no `preventDefault`. One notch is one line (a line- or page-mode event is one line in its direction; pixel deltas, a trackpad's, add up, 100 CSS pixels a line). The carry takes only events the engine takes (the page asks with `dy` 0 first, and cancels only those), and is dropped when the direction turns, at a wheel the engine does not take, when the pointer leaves the message window or the canvas, and at a key; Ctrl-wheel is the browser's zoom. Over a window in front of the message window, a window's frame or a gap, the page sends the rest pixel (`pixelAt` with `held`, through `layoutToGame` / `wideToGame`), which is outside the rect. Whole-screen frames: in `game` none covers the rect in the port (2026-10-28): the defeat (`actors/turn.rs:95-104`, `darkness`) prints its lines in the message window over a full redraw of the usual screen, and the end sequence (the launcher's `end`) is not ported (`main_loop.rs:246`); the gate is the program and the rect.
5. **New text while scrolled back does not move the view.** At each publish the offset grows by the lines that scrolled off since the last one, so the same lines stay in view; only the thumb moves (the history grew). The thumb above the bottom is the sign that newer lines follow. The view leaves the history only by the player (6) or the wheel and the bar; nothing the engine adds to the input moves it: M4's travel step (spec 21, `Engine::travel_press`) is queued for the game as the player's keys are but does not go through `View::input`, so a travel walks on with the view where it is (opus S1, round 4: round 4 sent it through, and each step returned the view to the live bottom).
6. **Leaving the history.** In `Engine::tick`, before it is queued for the game: a key press, a key repeat (K2's paced press, `InputEvent::Paced`, spec 19 O1c, included: `View::input` calls `leave()` for it as for a key) or typed text, and a pointer press (either button) anywhere but on the scrollbar, set the offset to 0 in that tick: the player's input only (the caller's events), not the travel's step (5). `View::input` runs before K2's take-back at the command prompt (`taken_back`), so an OS repeat of a direction key the prompt takes back, never queued, still returns the view to the live bottom. A move without a button, a release and the keys the page keeps for itself (the speed keys) do not. A press inside the message window off the scrollbar while scrolled back (no button held before it) returns to the live bottom and is not the game's (the dispatcher's decision, 2026-10-28: a click on the history must not act on the live window; the history's keywords are kept with their lines but a press on one is not routed, 10). A press with a button already held (the game's, a walk) is the game's whatever it lands on: the game sees every change of the buttons it holds.
7. **The scrollbar.** Drawn only while the offset is not 0, inside the window's last cell column (Original x 304..=311), over the window's 80 rows:
   - the track: a dotted 1-pixel line at the column's x 6 (Original x 310), every other row, in the text ink;
   - the thumb: 3 pixels wide at the column's x 5..=7 (Original x 309..=311), solid, in the text ink; its length is the visible fraction of the 80-pixel track, rows / (history + rows), at least 8 pixels; its top is (80 − length) × (lines above the view) / (history);
   - both are drawn only in the column's cells that show one colour (no letter, whatever the paper under them is): a cell that shows a letter keeps it whole, and the bar yields to it.

   Why thin and yielding: a scrollbar in its own cell column would hide the 17th cell, which holds text on about one row in eleven (facts); the arrow glyphs need a cell each. Why it yields to a letter cell instead of drawing on its paper pixels: in the HD font mode a cell is drawn in the HD font only when all 64 of its pixels are its glyph's (`u6-render/src/hd.rs:679-715`); a bar pixel in it would turn that letter back to the pixel font. The ink rule matches the game's own 1-pixel rules on the paper.
8. **The text area does not narrow.** The game wraps in `end_line` at its window's width, 17 (`ui/print.rs:200-216`), and the view never touches the window record or the line: live text always wraps as the original's. The view shows kept lines as they were wrapped.
9. **The bar's gestures.** The bar's hit area is the last cell column over the window's rows, only while it is drawn. A gesture starts only at a press with no button held before it (6). A left press on the thumb grabs it: moves with the button held set the thumb's top, and the offset follows (the inverse of 7); a left press on the track above the thumb shows a window of rows (10) older, below it 10 newer, at most to the live bottom. The gesture lasts until every button is up, wherever the pointer goes; a key, a repeat or text during it returns to the live bottom and ends the drag, and the press stays the view's until the buttons are up. Its events still reach the game, with no button held: the pointer sprite follows the pointer (it reads the queued positions, `engine.rs:217`), and the game sees moves only. While a gesture lasts, the platforms pace the steps as with a button held (`Engine::history_holding`, spec 06's 1×), up to the step that takes its release: a release queued paces that step as released, as a game press does (`u6-web` `pointer_held`). The page releases the buttons for the game when the canvas loses the pointer capture with a button down (`lostpointercapture`) as at a cancel (`pointercancel`), through one helper (`releaseUnfinished`): during a read by click (spec 22 Contract 8, `Game::reading_by_click`, no window drag) at frame pixel (-1, -1), off every target, else at the pointer's place; after it, the buttons it released stay up for the game while held, each until an event with it up or a new press on the canvas (`releasedHeld`, a mask of them; a second release while one of them is still held adds its own), so a press the game saw end does not start again where the pointer comes back with the button held; a button pressed after it (a chorded press, which the browser sends as a move, never a `pointerdown`) is the game's (opus N2, round 4: round 4 kept every button up until all were). New text during a thumb drag: the publish anchors the view (5), then the drag's next move sets the offset from the thumb's place again, so the view moves by the number of lines kept since: exactly that many at the bound (`new_text_during_a_thumb_drag_at_the_bound_moves_the_view_by_the_new_lines`), up to that many below it (derived from `View::drag`, not measured: the thumb's place is then a fraction of a history that grew), and the thumb follows the pointer; no count is frozen for the drag (text during a drag is rare, the dispatcher's decision 2026-10-28).
10. **Keyword spans: D1's, kept with the line.** D2 makes no spans of its own (round 2 deleted its alert-ink runs: they counted space cells in the alert ink, which the more prompt's blank row draws, and kept fragments of overdrawn keywords, against spec 22 Contract 4). At both scroll sites (`ui/print.rs` `end_line`, `ui/keyread.rs` `echo`) `talk::scroll` (spec 22) moves the window's spans up a row and returns those of the top row, and `scrollback::scrolled(ui, fb, &w, spans)` keeps them with the line (`Line::spans`, row 0, made to their length; another window's scroll keeps none). A span is kept only while each of its cells shows its drawing or that drawing inverse (`scrollback::as_drawn`), and a kept span's cells take no patch: the keyword under the pointer is drawn inverse (spec 22 Contract 6) and T1 does not cool it at the scroll, so the history keeps it as drawn, in its ink (`a_keyword_drawn_inverse_under_the_pointer_is_kept_in_its_ink`). A span drawn over (a window over the message window draws while the spans are not updated: `print::cell`, `ui/print.rs:39-44`, updates them only while the message window is current, and `scrollback::note` records any window's cell in the rect) is not kept, and its cells take their patch as any other (`a_keyword_drawn_over_is_not_kept_and_its_cells_take_their_patch`; opus N1, round 4: round 4 skipped every span cell, which hid such a patch). So, derived from `as_drawn` and not measured, is a span over shadow rows another window's scroll cleared (its cells blank in the shadow, its glyph still on the frame): round 4 kept it and lost its pixels; round 5 keeps them as patch. The stricter rule keeps 1's colour-exact claim with the hover its one exception, and keeps 11's bound: a kept span's cells still take no patch. A rest keeps its link (`joined`) only where the line kept before it holds a continued span of its ink (`scrollback::linked`), as spec 22 links a keyword's parts on two rows (`is_first` needs `continued`), never on `joined` alone (T1 note D2-2): on a kept line, `joined` means linked to the line kept before it, a narrower meaning than on the live window's spans, where it records what the wrap dropped. `continued` keeps its live meaning (the span holds its row's last drawn cell, where the wrap broke the keyword), so a reader that walks a kept keyword forward goes from a continued span to its rest as spec 22's `is_rest` does (`ui/talk.rs:63-65`: `joined` set, its ink, column 0 of the next kept line), never on `continued` alone (opus N5). A press on a kept keyword is not routed: a press in the window while scrolled back returns to live (6), and while scrolled back no keyword of the live window is a target (2).
11. **Memory.** A line of the window's 17 cells holds 102 bytes of cells, its patch 4 bytes a pixel (made to its length), its keyword spans (each span 56 bytes, derived from its fields: two vectors and four bytes, not printed by a test; and 2 bytes a cell, made to their length), and a shared header of 96 bytes (its two counts and the line's three vectors); the byte figures are the 64-bit host's, where the tests measure them (wasm32's pointers and lengths are half as wide, so the page's figures are smaller). A kept keyword's cells take no patch (10), so a line with spans holds less than one all patch: a span of k cells adds at most 56 + 2k + 2 bytes and takes 256k bytes of patch away. Typical: 500 lines with no patch, 104 932 bytes (`history_keeps_…`, printed); the real-data receipt's largest history, 17 750 bytes (round 2, before the spans), 20 490 bytes with them (round 5, both builds). Worst: every pixel of every line patch (a row the game filled in a colour the cells do not draw), 1088 pixels a line, 4 550 bytes a line, 2 280 932 bytes at the bound (`a_bound_of_lines_all_patch_keeps_the_stated_size`, printed, measured with `Scrollback::heap_bytes`): 500 lines, the deque's capacity 512 × 8 (it doubles up to 512 at line 257 and never needs more: at most 501 lines at once), the shadow's 10 rows (10 × 80 bytes and 10 × 102 of cells) and the token (16). With the view: the view keeps the lines as of the last publish, those the history dropped since included, and the history goes on keeping new ones up to the next publish, so the two together hold at most two bounds of distinct lines (500 each), the view's vector (its storage requested with `reserve_exact` for the most lines one publish took, at most 500 × 8 = 4 000 bytes requested; `reserve_exact` does not promise the allocator gives no more, so this ceiling is requested storage and the measured figures are the 64-bit host's toolchain's, where the capacity equalled the request, gpt a: `the_view_adds_only_what_the_history_no_longer_holds` checks it right after each publish while the history grows to the bound and past it, more than 400 of them, and after another history) and, before the first publish of another history, the view's own token (16): 2 280 932 + 500 × 4 550 + 4 000 + 16 = 4 559 948 bytes at most in requested storage, all patch (about 0.2 MB with no patch); measured 4 559 932: the history 2 280 932, the view 2 279 000 (`the_history_and_the_view_together_hold_at_most_two_bounds`: a bound published, then a bound more with no publish, the token shared; `Engine::history_heap_bytes` = `Scrollback::heap_bytes` + `View::heap_bytes`, which counts the view's vector, its token when it is not the history's, and each line of it the history no longer holds, each once: right after a publish the view adds its vector alone, gpt b). Beside these, while scrolled back with HD on, `Engine::with_view_frame` keeps one frame copy (the published frame's pixels, 320 × 200 bytes in Original and 912 × 408 in Wide, its palette and its source plane, one `Src` a pixel), made only while the offset is not 0 and dropped at the live bottom; it goes away at the M3 merge (12). The bound is kept as stated, not capped: the patch is what makes a kept line colour-exact, and the lines are shared, so neither a state copy nor the view copies them. Patch sources (a limitation, stated): a patch pixel is drawn back in its colour alone, naming the paper at its new place as `scroll_rect` renames a scrolled pixel (`Paper::over`) and no image elsewhere, so a patch pixel of a tile, a portrait or a glyph would show its classic colour in HD. None was seen: with the source plane on, every one of the receipt's 72 patch pixels named the paper on the live frame (a probe, counts only).
12. **Merge plan (D1, M3).** D2 merges after D1 (D1 merged into integ/r1027 17c09b6; D2's round 4 merges it in, 54db292). The second of D2 and M3 to merge makes one composition: published frame → view (kept lines from the view's lines, live rows from the published frame) → menu → sprite, into `shown`, and the HD key includes the view stamp; D2's separate HD frame (`Engine::with_view_frame`, which copies the published frame and its source plane at each publish while scrolled back) goes away then.

## Vertical trace (one scenario per section group; parameter arrows are as built)

### S-A: a line scrolls off and is kept (game draw path)

0. **Client surface.** None: the game prints.
1. **Entry.** `print::end_line` (`ui/print.rs:194`) at the row past the window's last, with `SCROLL` set; or `keyread::echo` (`ui/keyread.rs:195`) at the end of a typed row.
2. **Input.** The current window `w: TextWindow`; the front buffer `fb`; the shadow `ui.scrollback`.
3. **Layer flow.**
   - 3a. Every cell drawn inside the Messages rect: `(code c, w.fg, w.bg, kind)` → `Scrollback::rows[row − 14].cells[col − 22]` (`scrollback::note`, called after the draw at the five sites).
   - 3b. Before `text::scroll_window(fb, &w, paper)`: `spans = talk::scroll(ui)` when `w` is the current Messages window (spec 22: the window's spans up a row, the top row's returned), else none; `scrollback::scrolled(ui, fb, &w, spans)`: if `w`'s rect is the Messages rect, `rows[0]` → `Line { cells, bg, spans: linked(the line kept before, spans), patch: fb row 112..119 pixels ≠ the cells' drawing, the spans' cells aside }` → `Scrollback::lines.push_back`, the oldest dropped past 500, `total += 1`; `rows` move up one, the last becomes blank with paper `w.bg`. Another window whose rect overlaps it clears the shadow (its next kept lines take their colours from the patch).
4. **Side effects.** `UiState::scrollback` only. No other state, no frame write.
5. **Error paths.** No font: nothing is drawn and nothing is noted. A window of another size than the shadow's: the shadow starts blank at the new size.
6. **Output.** `Scrollback::lines`, `Scrollback::total`.

### S-B: the wheel shows older lines (view path)

0. **Client surface.** `site/app.js`: a `wheel` event on the canvas → Ctrl (or no game): the carry dropped, the browser's → `deltaY` 0: the browser's, nothing else → `wheelPixel(e)` (`pixelAt(e.clientX, e.clientY, box, true)`, as `pointerPixel` with `held`) → game pixel (x, y) → `Game::wheel(x, y, 0)`: false → the carry dropped, the event the browser's; true → `preventDefault`, `wheelLines(e)` → `dy` (decision 4) → `Game::wheel(x, y, dy)` when not 0.
1. **Entry.** `Game::wheel` (`u6-web/src/lib.rs`) → `Engine::wheel(x as i16, y as i16, dy)`.
2. **Input.** (x, y) inside 176..=311 × 112..=191 (the Messages record × 8), program `game`, else false and nothing changes.
3. **Layer flow.** `dy` → `View::offset = clamp(offset − dy, 0, shown)` where `shown` = `View::lines.len()`, the lines kept as of the last publish; a change bumps `View::stamp`. `Engine::wheel` composes at once (trace deviation); `Engine::tick` composes whenever `stamp ≠ shown_stamp`: `shown.sync_from(published)` → `scrollback::draw_view(&ui, &view, &published, &mut shown)` → sprite → `shown_version += 1`. `draw_view`: view row r (0..10) shows combined line `shown − offset + r` of (`View::lines` ++ the published window's rows): a kept line → fill the row with its paper (`text::fill_rect`, naming the paper) → each cell through `draw_char` / `draw_typed` / `draw_symbol` → the patch; a live row → the published row r − (that index − shown) copied, paper sources renamed for the new row as `scroll_rect` does; then the bar (decision 7).
4. **Side effects.** `View` only. `State` unchanged.
5. **Error paths.** Offset already at an end: clamped, no stamp, nothing redrawn. No kept line: nothing.
6. **Output.** `Engine::frame()` (RGBA path: `Game::present` → `redraw`, `u6-web/src/lib.rs:966`, `:1728`) with the view; HD: `HdView::present` keys on `(published_version, view stamp)` and composes `Engine::with_view_frame` (`hd.rs:150`).
7. **Observability.** None logged (the lines are game text).

### S-C: the player's input returns to the live bottom

1. **Entry.** `Engine::tick(input)` (`engine.rs:776`), each event before it is queued.
3. **Layer flow.** `Key` / `Repeat` / `Paced` / `Text` → `offset = 0` (a gesture becomes `Gesture::Held`), before K2's take-back; `Pointer { buttons }` with a button newly pressed and one held before, or outside the message window, or at offset 0 → `offset = 0`, queued as given; with none held before, inside the window off the bar, at offset ≠ 0 → `offset = 0`, `Gesture::Held`, queued with `buttons = 0` until every button is up.
6. **Output.** The next composition draws no view: the frame is the live one.

### S-D: the scrollbar gestures

1. **Entry.** `Engine::tick(input)`, `InputEvent::Pointer`.
3. **Layer flow.** Offset ≠ 0, no button held before, a left press inside the last cell column × rows → on the thumb: `Gesture::Thumb { grab: y − top }`; above / below it: `offset ± 10` (clamped); a right press there: `Gesture::Held`; the event queued with `buttons = 0`. While the gesture lasts: `top = clamp(y − grab, 0, 80 − len)` → `offset = shown − round(top × shown / (80 − len))` (Thumb, left held); queued with `buttons = 0`; every button up ends it.
6. **Output.** As S-B.

### S-E: new text arrives while scrolled back

3. **Layer flow.** At a publish (`engine.rs:828` `presented`): the same token and the same total → nothing; else `new = total − view.published_total` in the same history (another token: none); `token`, `published_total = total`, `View::lines` = the shared kept lines now; offset ≠ 0 → `offset = min(offset + new, shown)`, or 0 with no `new`, stamp bumped. A source-only publish (`Io::present_sources`) leaves the view as it is.
6. **Output.** The same kept lines in view; the thumb shorter and higher.

## Trace deviations

- S-B §3: `Engine::wheel` composes the shown frame at once (`Engine::compose_shown`), so the frame a page draws right after the wheel shows it; `Engine::tick` still composes whenever the view's stamp moved (the bar's gestures, the anchoring of S-E).
- Decision 7 as first written drew the bar only over cells of the window's paper colour; the oldest kept lines can sit on another colour (a row cleared before the paper was drawn, a window cleared in another paper), so the rule is a cell of one colour, whatever it is. The letter rule is unchanged.
- S-A §6: the newest kept line never shows on the published frame's last row (the newline scrolls before the next text shows there), so a test reading the window over the run sees each kept line on the frames as one of rows 0..8.
- Round 2 (2026-10-28): the browser check of the HD frame, once its digest left out the bar's column, no longer saw the history in the HD layout and Wide: with the HD font on, the text is drawn by the page from the HD frame's list of cells (spec 11), not in the HD frame's pixels, so round 1's check had passed on the bar's pixels alone. The digest takes the listed cells in the window's text columns too. The RGBA check now reads the scrolled-back window with the pointer's sprite out of it, as at the live read (the sprite had made "changed" pass with no history row drawn, W5).
- Round 2: the view's lines as of the last publish are its own (`View::lines`, shared with the history), no longer counted back from the history's lines now: at the bound, lines kept and dropped after a publish had shifted the view on a source-only publish (and fired the engine's colour assert, `engine.rs:887`).
- Round 4 (the T1 merge): the integration notes put the keyword gate in `talk::target_at`; it is in `talk::pointer`, its one caller outside the tests, which has the `Io` (`target_at` takes the state alone and five spec 22 tests call it at the live bottom). `another_history_returns_…` replaces the state through `main_loop::take_over` (opus N3), whose new state's window starts at its top row: the new history prints nine lines more for the same counts.
- Round 3: another history is told by a token (`Generation`), no longer by finding the view's newest line in the history where the count put it: a new history a bound longer than the view's lines put that place out of reach and was taken for the same one (the view stayed scrolled back into lines it never had). The token is minted by construction (every new `Scrollback` and every copy), not bumped at each replacement site, so no site can forget it.
- Round 5: M4's travel step is added after the caller's events in `Engine::tick` and queued as they are, but not through `View::input` (round 4 concatenated it with them, so each step left the history, opus S1). The `Io`'s copy of the offset is written once a tick, before the game's pass (the writes after the publish and at the wheel were never read: the game's one pass is `Engine::tick`'s poll, after the input's write, and `talk::pointer` is called from the read loop alone, `ui/keyread.rs`; the page's `Game::history_offset` asks the view). A keyword span is kept only while its cells show its drawing or its inverse, so a real patch under a span drawn over is no longer skipped (opus N1).
